   transportation problems (T.P.). This paper proposes a new (V.A.M.) modification to find (IBFS) of (T.P.). This revised

                                                               INTRODUCTION

    Many companies and production industries face many difficulties of economic improvements in our daily lives,
such as reducing costs to increase profits. Transportation models are an example of these rooted financial
optimizations. The (TP) is a particular class of linear programming problems (LPP). The (TP) basically cares about
the most efficient way to move a product from several factories or plants (referred to as supply origins) to a variety
of warehouses or customers (referred to as demand destinations). Until the best shipment plan is chosen, one that
minimizes total transportation costs while meeting both (supply) and (demand). Hitchcock [1] introduced the
fundamental transportation problem in 1941, while Koopmans [2] studied the subject further from 1947-1949.
There are two stages to finding the (OS) to (TP). The (IBFS) is obtained in the first step by using any of the various
traditional approaches, such as the “North-West Corner Method” (NWCM), “Least Cost Method” (LCM), and
“Vogel’s Approximation Method” (VAM). The “Modified Distribution Method” (MODI) was used to find the (OS)
in the second stage. In new research, the “Stepping Stone Method” was designed by Charnes and Cooper [3] to find
the (OS) for (IBFS). Goyal [4] proposed a study to solve the unbalanced transportation problem by Modification
(VAM). In 2002, Ping Ji [5] presented his study “The dual-matrix approach to the transport problem.” In 2006,
Moriah and Garg [6] Introduced a new technique to find the best assignment table in administrative systems. In
2010, Pandian et al. [7] suggested different ways to find the (OS) to (TP) directly. Samuel and Venkachalapati [8]
recently introduced a new technique to modify fuzzy transport problems (VAM). In 2019, Amaliah [9] proposed a
new approach to determine the initial basic feasible solution to (TP). In this study, we present a proposed
modification of (VAM) called (MS) to obtain (IBFS) of (TP). The results are close to (OS) (and occasionally more
petite than the optimal solution) and better or equal to the results of the solution according to (VAM). Anyway, the
outcomes of our proposed method outperform the traditional methods (NWCM) and (LCM).

     Step1: Create a transportation table based on (T.P.) information (if not provided). Ensure that aggregate supply
meets aggregate demand; if not, balance (T.P.).
     Step 2: a) Select the smallest cost element in the first column of (T.P.) table and subtract that cost element from
each element in that column. Repeat this process for all columns in (T.P.) table. As a result, each column in the new
table will contain at least one zero. This new table is referred to as the (reduced column matrix).
     b) Select the smallest cost element in the first row of (reduced column matrix). Subtract it from each element in
that row. Repeat this row in the new table will contain at least one zero. This new table is referred to as the (reduced
row matrix).
     Step 3: Locate the locations of the zero cells in the original schedule depending on the location of the zero cells
in the (reduced row matrix table).

                                                   CONCLUSION

   One of the essential stages of getting the (O.S.) for (T.P.) is to find (IBFS). It is worth noting that (V.A.M.) is the
best way to solve (T.P.) among the classic methods. In this article, a new approach (M.T.) is proposed for finding
(IBFS) of (T.P.). The results we reached using (M.T.) compared with the solutions provided by the classical
methods (NWCM), (MCM), and (V.A.M.). The (M.T.) results are the best and sometimes equal or less than the
(O.S.). The recommended solution has a reasonable possibility of resolving (T.P.). This technique may be preferred
over current methods.
