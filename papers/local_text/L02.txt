Background

    The Transportation Problem (TP) is an optimization model that focuses on delivering goods from supply sources to demand des­
tinations at the minimum possible cost [1,2]. It is usually expressed in the form of a cost matrix, where each entry represents the
expense of transporting a single unit of goods from one origin to a destination [3]. Hitchcock first introduced the idea of TP in 1941 [4].
Since then, numerous approaches have been proposed to make transportation more cost-efficient. In practice, TP has been applied in
many fields, such as task scheduling and vehicle routing, helping organizations cut costs while ensuring that deliveries remain timely
and efficient [5].
    The solution process usually begins with an Initial Basic Feasible Solution (IBFS) [6], which satisfies all problem constraints and
serves as the starting point for optimization. The quality of the IBFS strongly affects the efficiency of reaching the optimal solution
through procedures such as Stepping Stone or Modified Distribution (MODI) [7]. Heuristics for IBFS are therefore evaluated by their
ability to generate solutions close to the optimum.
    Several heuristic methods have been developed for IBFS, including the Northwest Corner Rule (NWC), Least Cost Method (LCM),
and Vogel’s Approximation Method (VAM) [8]. Over time, researchers have proposed modifications and other alternatives to improve
performance, such as the Total Opportunity Cost Matrix (TOCM) method [1], the Juman–Hoque Method (JHM) [2], and the Bil­
qis–Chastine–Erma (BCE) method [9]. BCE was later extended into the Supply Selection Method (SSM), which modified allocation
rules when supply exceeded demand [10].
    Although these heuristics contribute helpful strategies, their results are not always consistent in producing low-cost IBFS. SSM, for
example, often performs well but still exhibits variability across cases. This motivates the development of the Rihan–Bilqis–Saikhu
Method (RBSM). RBSM builds upon SSM by incorporating the total cost-supply of each row and refining the rules for reallocating
excess supply from the first least cost (FLC) to the second least cost (SLC).

Method details

    The Rihan–Bilqis–Saikhu Method (RBSM) is a heuristic designed to generate an Initial Basic Feasible Solution (IBFS) for the
Transportation Problem (TP). It builds upon the Supply Selection Method (SSM) but introduces modifications to improve consistency
in cost efficiency, such as:

1. It incorporates the total cost of each supply row into the decision process.
2. It refines the allocation transfer rule, where excess supply is reallocated from the first least cost (FLC) to the second least cost (SLC).

    Table 1 provides a more detailed explanation of these modifications, outlines the specific steps, and differences compared to SSM.

transportation cost Cij for all cells in this dummy sector is set to zero. The RBSM algorithm then proceeds normally, treating the zero
costs as regular values for calculating the Total Cost (TC) and cost differentials.

method were compared against the known optimal costs. Detailed IBFS Results compared with the optimal solution of each method
and testcase shown in (Supplementary Table 1).

representing the number of cases in which a method achieved the optimal solution across datasets of different sizes. The results, shown
in Table 9, cover seven matrix dimensions. This distribution highlights how the success rate in reaching optimal solutions varies
according to problem size.
    Table 10 presents the number of optimal solutions obtained by each method along with their respective Accuracy Percentage (Ap),
determined according to Eq. (8). The results show clear differences in accuracy across methods, with some reaching the optimal so­
lution less frequently while others performed more consistently. As shown in Fig. 2, Accuracy Percentage of each method, providing a
visual comparison of performance. From these results, it can be observed that RBSM achieved the highest level of accuracy among the
tested methods.

    To evaluate the performance differences rigorously, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test was conducted comparing the deviation per­
centages of RBSM against the reference method, SSM. The test focused on the 12 cases where the methods yielded different results (in
30 out of 42 cases, both methods achieved identical solutions).
    The analysis revealed a strong positive trend favoring RBSM, with Sum of Positive Ranks of 55, significantly outweighing the Sum
of Negative Ranks of 23. While the resulting p-value (0.1047) stands slightly above the conventional 0.05 threshold due to the reduced

Limitations

   Similar to its reference method, the Supply Selection Method (SSM), the proposed Rihan–Bilqis–Saikhu Method (RBSM) has so far
been evaluated only on transportation problems with fixed supply, demand, and cost parameters. Its applicability to cases involving
uncertainty and dynamic changes using fuzzy logic has not yet been investigated.
